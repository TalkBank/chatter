## Description

The first compound part must contain spoken material before the joining
marker (`+`). A stress marker alone does not supply that material. Valid
compounds have the form `left+right`, with spoken material in each part.

## Expected Behavior

The literal leading-plus example retains its parse-stage E316 claim. The
stress-only first-part example parses without recovery and reports E232 at
validation, with the original word span and byte-exact serialization. Stress
does not supply a missing lexical constituent. CHECK 21-Sep-2026 accepts the
control and rejects the mutation with 11; this is per-shape evidence, not an
equivalence between every CHECK 11 case and E232.

## CHAT Rule

<https://talkbank.org/0info/manuals/CHAT.html#Compounds> documents the
`left+right` compound-word form. Supply spoken material before the first
joining marker or remove the marker; adding stress or another nonlexical marker
does not repair an empty first part. Literal word-initial `+` is also invalid,
although the grammar may reject it before word validation.

## Diagnostic ownership

The typed validator tracks whether the initial or a joined compound part has
spoken material. A join before the initial part has spoken material reports
E232. Empty middle or final parts report [E233](E233.md). Stress and other
nonlexical markers do not turn an empty part into a spoken part.

This is not a requirement that every invalid compound produce E232 at the
same stage. A literal leading plus can be rejected by the grammar and reported
as E316 before a Word exists. The stress-only initial part is represented by
the typed word and reaches model validation. Both reject the unsupported
structure without a parallel parser or diagnostic-specific grammar exception.

## Historical scope

A September 7 audit described the former first-element-only check and proposed
grammar changes to expose it. That implementation and those recommendations
were superseded by the September 23 compound-part state machine. The retained
literal-plus example records subsumption; the legal control and deliberate
first-part deletion demonstrate the current model rule. Finite recovery probes
do not prove other recovery states impossible and do not justify removing
recovery handling.
