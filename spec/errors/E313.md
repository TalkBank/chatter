Inside a group the stray `(` survives as an ERROR node that is a direct child
of the group's `contents` list. The one typed `contents` walker
(`main_tier/structure/contents.rs`) classifies an ERROR item there exactly
as on the tier body, and the shared unclosed-delimiter pattern names it.
The span is the single `(` byte.

The separator after the `(` is what makes the shape reachable, and it is the
whole trick: a `(` followed by a word segment is recovered as a `shortening`
with a MISSING `)` instead, which reports E342 plus the model-level E231
(`<hello (word> [!] .` does exactly that), so the `(` has to be unable to open
a shortening at all. A comma, a semicolon or a colon after it does that.

[!] .`, validates clean, so the unclosed parenthesis is this example's only

## Description

An opening parenthesis in word content has no closing partner. Parentheses
in CHAT mark the omitted part of a noncompleted word and untimed pauses, and
both forms are balanced.

## Expected Behavior

E313 is reported when tree-sitter recovery leaves the stray `(` as a bare
ERROR node in a `contents` list, which is the shape a group (`<...>`), a
quotation, or a pho or sin group produces. The group's content walker hands
that node to `unexpected_node_error`, and the classifier in
`tree_parsing/helpers.rs` names the unclosed parenthesis. The third example is
that case, and E313 is the only diagnostic it raises.

Two other recoveries claim the parenthesis first, so E313 is not the verdict
everywhere an unbalanced `(` appears. Where the `(` can open a `shortening`
(it is followed by a word segment) inside a group, tree-sitter inserts a
MISSING `)` and the file reports E342 with the model-level E231 rather than
E313. In bare word position on the main tier the recovery region goes to the
main-tier word classifier, which has no parenthesis test, so it reports E316.
A balanced noncompletion `(be)cause` parses as one word.

## CHAT Rule

The CHAT manual's "Noncompletion of a Word" code puts the omitted material in
parentheses inside the word:
<https://talkbank.org/0info/manuals/CHAT.html#Noncompletion_Code>; pauses
are `(.)`, `(..)`, `(...)` or a timed `(2.5)`
(<https://talkbank.org/0info/manuals/CHAT.html#Pause_Marker>). CLAN CHECK
reports an unmatched `(` as its error 28.

## Notes

- The reachable shape is narrow, and the examples above map it: the `(` must
  be unable to open a `shortening`, and it must sit inside a bracketed
  `contents` rather than directly on the main tier.
- Word-position unbalanced parentheses stay on E316. Moving them onto E313
  would mean giving the main-tier word-content recovery classifier a
  parenthesis balance test, which is a parser change rather than a spec one;
  until then example 1 records the subsumption as the current state, not as
  intended behaviour.
