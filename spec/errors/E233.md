## Description

Compound markers (`+`) must connect two non-empty parts. Adjacent compound markers
(`un++do`) create an empty part between them, which is invalid.

A trailing `+` can also reach word-level validation: `hey+ .` leaves the
compound without a right-hand part (CHECK 67). The earlier claim that the
grammar prevents every trailing marker was too broad. Both internal and
trailing empty parts belong to this rule.

Prosodic markers do not supply lexical parts. The additional control/mutation
pairs delete the spoken material between two joins or after the last join,
leaving a stress marker in place. Both mutations report E233; the final-part
mutation also retains E245 because its stress has no following spoken material.
CHECK 21-Sep-2026 accepts these controls and both mutations: these are grounded
Chatter validity checks, not claims that CHECK enforces every empty-part shape.

## Expected Behavior

The trailing-marker example parses into a word and validation reports E233.
The adjacent-marker example is rejected during parsing with E233. The
observations record those different stages; neither is valid CHAT merely
because a partial or complete word can be retained.

**Trigger**: Adjacent compound markers or a trailing marker create an empty part.
