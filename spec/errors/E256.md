## Description

A curly single quotation mark (U+2018 or U+2019) is used as a word character.
CHAT requires the ASCII apostrophe; the curly form (typically a typographic
apostrophe introduced by autocorrect or ASR) is not a legal word character.
Mirrors CLAN CHECK errors 138 (U+2019) and 139 (U+2018).

## Expected Behavior

The grammar must not treat the curly single quote as a legal word character.
The parser should recognize it as an illegal-character node and report E256
rather than silently absorbing it into the word token. The curly double quotes
(U+201C/U+201D) remain legal only as quotation-group bookends, never as word
characters.

The shortening mutations replace only the letter in valid `wan(t)` with U+2018
or U+2019. Each produces E256 during parsing, with the quote nested inside a
larger CST `ERROR` region. The legal control round-trips byte-exactly; the
invalid variants do not, because recovery does not preserve that malformed
shortening as valid content. This recovery path must retain the specific quote
diagnostic rather than treating the enclosing parentheses as permission.

## CHAT Rule

CHAT word text uses ASCII punctuation; curly typographic single quotes are not
legal word characters and should be the ASCII apostrophe. CLAN CHECK 138/139.
CHAT manual: https://talkbank.org/0info/manuals/CHAT.html

CHECK (21-Sep-2026) accepts the shortening control and reports 139 for its
left-quote mutation and 138 for its right-quote mutation. Source inspection
matches the observations: `check.cpp::isBadQuotes` (2906–2913) recognizes the
two UTF-8 byte sequences, and the main-tier word scan in `check_CheckWords`
(3197–3199) emits the returned code. The sensible shared policy is character
validity, including inside parenthesized word material; Chatter expresses the
same fault as E256 without adopting CHECK's byte-scanning implementation.

## Notes

- Both parsers reject the curly single quote. The tree-sitter grammar recognizes
  it as a dedicated `illegal_curly_quote` node (excluded from `word_segment` via
  the symbol registry) and the parser emits E256 with a precise span; the re2c
  lexer emits a recognized `IllegalCurlyQuote` token that the file-level parser
  reports as E256. Ordinary surrounding words can survive so validation
  continues; malformed surrounding constructs may also be lost during recovery.
  Neither backend's recovered output certifies the original text as valid.
