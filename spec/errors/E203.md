## Description

Word contains an invalid or undeclared `@` form type marker, OR carries more
than one `@` suffix.

**A word may carry at most ONE `@` suffix.** Ruled by the maintainer on
2026-08-27, asked because CLAN CHECK accepts multiple suffixes and chatter does
not: "Multiple suffixes might make logical sense, but it is computationally
messy. So, let's disallow that." He added that the combination is usually
incoherent anyway: calling a child-invented form a Spanish word "is wrong in
the first place".

So `dog@b@c` is invalid for stacking two form markers, and `gumma@c@s:spa` is
invalid too, even though the child-invented marker `@c` and the language suffix
`@s:spa` are each perfectly good on their own. Main-tier words carrying two `@`
runs number ZERO across the ~106,000 kept files; the shape occurs only inside
`%com` and `%exp` free text.

**This is a DOCUMENTED DIVERGENCE from CLAN CHECK**, which passes these files.
chatter is deliberately stricter, on the ruling above.

## Expected Behavior

The appropriate error should be reported; which stage catches it is observed in the snapshot, not declared.

## CHAT Rule

The CHAT manual documents `@` word suffixes (form types, e.g. `@b` babbling,
`@c` child-invented form, `@s:xxx` language-tagged) individually; it does not
document stacking two of them on one word, and nothing in the manual's word
grammar admits a second `@` run. See
<https://talkbank.org/0info/manuals/CHAT.html#Word> for the form-type suffix
list. Absent a documented multi-suffix grammar, chatter treats a second `@`
run as invalid rather than silently accepting whichever combination happens to
parse, per the 2026-08-27 maintainer ruling recorded above.

## Notes

- The two examples above pin two different failure shapes under the same
  code: a single word carrying two DECLARED form markers (`dog@b@c`), and a
  word carrying one declared form marker plus a declared language suffix
  (`gumma@c@s:spa`): both individually valid, invalid only in combination.
- This is a documented divergence from CLAN CHECK (see Description); do not
  treat CHECK's silence on files like these as evidence chatter is wrong.
