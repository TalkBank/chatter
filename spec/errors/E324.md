## Description

An utterance contains material no rule of the grammar or of the recovery
classifiers recognizes, here a stray closing brace. Braces in CHAT belong to
long-feature and nonvocal scopes (`&{l=...` ... `&}l=...`), and a bare `}`
belongs to nothing.

## Expected Behavior

Whole-file parsing recovers the main-tier content, and the classifier
reports the unparsable region as E316 (unparsable content); E324 is the
fragment parser's last-resort verdict for an ERROR node that matched no
named rule, and the whole-file route never reaches it. Well-formed content
parses without either code.

**Trigger**: See example above

## CHAT Rule

The manual has no rule for a stray brace as such; it defines what braces
mean in the "Scoped Symbols" section
(<https://talkbank.org/0info/manuals/CHAT.html#Scoped_Symbols>), and
anything outside those forms is not CHAT.

## Notes

- The registry summary "unrecognized error in utterance content" is
  accurate: this is a catch-all with no rule of its own, and the spec cannot
  give it a boundary beyond "the grammar did not accept it". A future fragment
  example (parsed through the utterance entry point) is the only way to
  demonstrate it as `violates`; the spec system parses whole files.
