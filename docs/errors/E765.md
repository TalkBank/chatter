# E765: separator glued to the following content

> ✅ Active; This check is active in the validator.

**Kind**: Invalidity

**Level**: utterance

**Status**: ✅ Active

## Description

A plain separator (`:` or `;`) and a pause (`(.)`, `(2.4)`) are free-standing
main-tier items. Whatever follows one of them starts a new item and must be
separated from it by a space:

```
*CHI:	I know : and then she left .
```

Written glued, `:and` or `;;` or `(.)dog` still parses, and it parses as two
items, so the missing space is invisible: nothing distinguishes a typo from
intent. This is the accidental-juxtaposition class, in the E749/E751/E757/E764
family, and this rule generalizes it to every separator.

**Only the FOLLOWING side is rejected, deliberately.** Glue on the preceding
side (`word↘`, `dog,`) is a documented, heavily-attested CHAT convention:
CA intonation arrows are utterance-level marks that attach to the word they
follow (30,790 letter-glued attestations in the kept corpus), and `dog,` is the
long-standing comma compromise. Those stay valid. A word glued to a colon
(`dog:`) is a third case that is not even two items: the colon fuses into the
word as prosodic lengthening.

**The CA marks and the overlap markers are OUT OF SCOPE, on corpus evidence.**
Cell 7 was ruled REJECT for the whole separator class on 2026-07-18, against
an estimate of roughly six affected files. Implementing that reading and
running the corpus differential (2026-07-29, 2,134-file stratified sample)
produced 270 new instances, about 13,500 corpus-wide, and every inspected one
was legitimate CA notation rather than a missing space:

- `y≡I≡⌈xxx⌉` (GCSAusE/38): `≡` is latching, "no gap between turns". It is
  written glued on both sides because that is what it encodes; spacing it
  would misstate the phenomenon.
- `⌊I don't know⇗⌋` (same file): the arrow attaches to the material it marks,
  directly before the overlap close.

Adjudicated UNINTENDED under the corpus-differential rule, so the rule ships
narrowed to `:`/`;`/pauses, where the differential is clean. Whether any CA
mark should additionally forbid trailing glue is an open question for the
corpus owner, with those receipts; the remaining marks (`„ ‡ ∞ ≈ ≋`) have not
been adjudicated against real data at all.

Comma-specific shapes keep their own codes and are not reported here as well:
`,dog` is E749 and `,,` is E258.

## CHAT Rule

Main-tier items are space-delimited. A plain `:`/`;` separator and a pause are
items, so the next item takes a space; the absence of one is invalid even
though the parse is unambiguous. The preceding side is exempt, because trailing
glue onto a word is documented CHAT convention for the CA marks and for the
comma.

Juxtaposition-matrix cell 7, ruled REJECT 2026-07-18 and shipped narrowed. The
live probe matrix (2026-07-29, all 144 ordered class pairs, plus a per-variant
sweep over all 15 `Separator` variants) settles the direction: every variant
accepted glue on BOTH sides before this rule, and the ruled exceptions are all
trailing, so a class-level rule in the other direction would have rejected
30,790 attested arrow usages. The corpus differential then settled the
membership, as described above.
